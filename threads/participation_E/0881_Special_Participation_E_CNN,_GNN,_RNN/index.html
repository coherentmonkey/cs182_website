Overview I used NotebookLM Studio to generate flashcards, quizzes, slides, and a mind map from my lecture materials. Among these, the flashcards were the most useful for quick terminology review, while the other Studio products were either redundant or too high-level for the kind of math-heavy practice this class expects. Flashcards: https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/833433a8-9e95-40e6-9a64-131481809b45?artifactId=122dc77b-aeb6-4413-a4e0-fd4aff2e4e95 Quiz: https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/833433a8-9e95-40e6-9a64-131481809b45?artifactId=60c41b53-8e5c-4981-8c31-4071c6b9438d Link to the project: https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/833433a8-9e95-40e6-9a64-131481809b45?authuser=1 Trace with my annotations: What I tried in NotebookLM I explored almost all the Studio functions, including Flashcards, Quiz, Slide Deck, Mind Map, Reports, and Infographic, to see which ones actually help with exam preparation. Takeaways Most helpful Studio products: The Flashcards were genuinely useful for reviewing key terminology and checking whether I could explain concepts in my own words. That’s the main reason I decided to share the flashcard artifact. I also tried the Quiz, but I felt it overlapped heavily with the flashcards. Many questions tested the same kind of knowledge. So after doing the flashcards, the quizzes didn’t add much extra value. Less helpful Studio products: The Mind Map gave a hierarchical overview of the material, but it stayed very high-level and didn’t help much with deeper understanding. The Reports did a decent job organizing key terms and concepts, but it still focused more on definitions than on math or derivations. The Slide Deck looked polished, but after I had already reviewed flashcards/quizzes, they didn’t add much. The Infographic was fun (I liked the visuals), but it didn’t contain much information that helped me study. Explain button (what worked and what didn’t): One feature I really liked was the “Explain” button built into flashcards and quizzes. It’s convenient because I don’t need to write a long prompt every time I’m confused. I can just click once and get an explanation. That said, the explanations often felt harder to read quickly compared to ChatGPT or Claude. ChatGPT and Claude usually format explanations with shorter sentences and clearer bullet points, which makes it easier to skim and grasp the key idea. Also, I noticed the Explain button usually focuses on explaining only the correct answer, which is often the part I already understand. What I was hoping for is an explanation of the wrong answer choices too: why they are wrong, and how you would revise them (or rewrite the sentence) to make them correct. But there doesn’t seem to be an easy one-click button for that, so I still ended up needing to manually ask follow-up questions or go back to my lecture notes (or the LaTeX summary I prepared in ChatGPT) when I wanted a clearer, more complete explanation. Pipeline Step 1: Prepare lecture notes as input. I started from lecture content (screenshots of slides with my notes), and I avoided uploading full PDFs when possible because the extra formatting sometimes makes content extraction less reliable. Step 2: Summarize in ChatGPT first. I used ChatGPT (GPT-5.1 Thinking) to produce a LaTeX-formatted lecture summary, since it is good at digesting screenshot text into a structured write-up. Step 3: Use NotebookLM as the “review generator.” I uploaded the LaTeX summary (for lectures 10-15, e.g., CNN/GNN/RNN topics) into NotebookLM as sources. Step 4: Generate Studio artifacts. I generated flashcards, quizzes, slides, and a mind map. Step 5: Study with flashcards/quizzes. The most useful part was going through flashcards (and sometimes the quiz), and clicking Explain on the ones I didn’t fully understand. The mind map review was optional and mostly just for a high-level check. Conclusions I would recommend using NotebookLM flashcards (and optionally the quiz) for core-idea / terminology review (especially if your goal is “given a term, can I explain what it means?”). However, for this class, I personally felt NotebookLM’s flashcards/quizzes were often too high-level and didn’t cover enough derivation-style or math-heavy practice. For more exam-like practice problems, Claude’s generated practice exam (from my other post #880) felt much closer to what we see in homework/discussion, and it helped more for deeper understanding. (However, it’s possible this is partly influenced by my sources. If the input sources to NotebookLM emphasize conceptual explanations more than derivations, the outputs will probably skew that way too.)
