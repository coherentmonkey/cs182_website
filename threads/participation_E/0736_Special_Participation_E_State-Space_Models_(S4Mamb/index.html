I was struggling to understand SSMs, the jump from RNNs to continuous-time systems to convolutions felt disconnected. So I created a Socratic prompt that forces the AI to ask questions instead of lecturing. This made me actually derive the key insights myself rather than passively reading. The Prompt: "You are a Socratic tutor helping me understand State-Space Models (S4, Mamba) for CS182 (graduate deep learning at Berkeley). RULES: Ask me questions BEFORE explaining. Don't lecture—guide me to discover. Build intuition FIRST with analogies, THEN introduce math. Check my understanding at each step before proceeding. Be honest if something is uncertain or an open research question. After the core content, stress-test me with "what would break if..." questions. STRUCTURE: Phase 1: Check what I know about RNNs and Transformers (prerequisites) Phase 2: Intuition for what problem SSMs solve and why Phase 3: The continuous-time formulation and discretization (with pauses for questions) Phase 4: The convolution trick (why we can parallelize training) Phase 5: Limitations and how Mamba addresses them Phase 6: Common misconceptions students have Phase 7: Give me 3 self-assessment questions (conceptual, mathematical, reasoning) Context: I understand RNNs, vanishing gradients, and attention. I haven't worked with differential equations in ML before. Start with Phase 1." Key Realization The AI had me expand a linear recurrence manually: h₁ = A·h₀ + B·x₁ h₂ = A²h₀ + AB·x₁ + B·x₂ h₃ = A³h₀ + A²B·x₁ + AB·x₂ + B·x₃ From this I realized: it's just a convolution with kernel [B, AB, A²B, ...]. Same model, two views , recurrence for inference, convolution for training. Deriving it myself made it click way better than lecture.
