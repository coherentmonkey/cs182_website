By Kelvin Li 2025-12-11 GPT
View on Ed Stem →
The optimizers part of the course (lectures 2-7) can feel very heavy and overwhelming. I find it useful to try and condense information as much as possible so I can focus on the right things. Often times such condensed information comes in the form of a cheat sheet. Although these are not allowed for the final, I find it useful for studying. Here were the steps I did: Step 1 — Compress the slides I asked the AI to turn the slide content into a very concise, high-yield cheat sheet: keep only essential concepts remove redundancy keep key equations + intuition organize clearly one-page style This helped turn dense content into something readable. Step 2 — Let the AI self-critique Next, I asked the AI to review and correct its own summary: fix inaccuracies add missing assumptions clean notation group related topics add a short “unifying principles” section Repeating this step improves both accuracy and clarity. Step 3 — Generate questions to find my gaps Then I asked the AI to create diagnostic questions to test deeper understanding: NTK vs feature learning width scaling + μP gradient/curvature effects what momentum can/can’t fix how Muon differs from true second-order methods I answered the questions myself, and the AI pointed out gaps + updated the cheat sheet. This made the notes personalized to my actual misunderstandings. Once again, this can be done repeatedly to keep refining the cheat sheet. Step 4 — Convert to LaTeX (optional) Once everything was refined, I had the AI output a LaTeX version so it looks clean for studying.
