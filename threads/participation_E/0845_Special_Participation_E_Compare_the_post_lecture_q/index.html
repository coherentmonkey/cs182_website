Special Participation E: Compare the post lecture quiz generated by two modes of ChatGPT

By Shuwei Yang 2025-12-11 GPT
View on Ed Stem →
I wanted to use an ChatGPT as a kind of “post-lecture reading + practice” tool for Lecture 5 (optimizers), not just a homework solver. I asked ChatGPT in two modes to generate a quiz plus answer key: Quiz 1 was made with a “deep research” style mode, and Quiz 2 was made with GPT-5.1 Thinking. My goal is to compare the "deep research" mode worth it, because it require ChatGPT Pro. Will the quality of quiz generate in that mode be better than basic ChatGPT 5.1? Quiz 1 feels more like an “advanced concepts” quiz. The questions form a clear chain: local linear view → signSGD and ℓ∞ → RMS/Xavier → spectral norm → Shampoo → μP → Muon. It really pushes you to connect these topics into one geometric story, which matches the spirit of the lecture. Quiz 2 is more mixed: the first few questions are basic (what is gradient descent, why do we need an optimizer, etc.), and only later it moves into the same advanced ideas. This makes Quiz 2 friendlier as a warm-up, but less focused as a single advanced reading substitute. There are also places where the model is a bit misleading or overconfident. Both quizzes describe Shampoo as if it literally takes an SVD of the gradient ∇W​ℓ=UΣVT and then updates with −ηUVT. That is a nice geometric picture, but not what the real algorithm does in practice (it uses Kronecker-factored second moments instead). Also, both answers talk about Muon giving “>10× speedups,” which is almost surely exaggerated compared to real papers. The qualitative ideas are right (geometry-aware updates, better scaling with μP), but the exact numbers should not be trusted. If I share this on Ed, my recommendation is: use Quiz 2’s early questions (Q1–Q4 or Q1–Q6) as a light review, then switch to Quiz 1 for the more serious, concept-heavy questions. The answer keys are helpful for building intuition, but they need a small “warning label” about the Shampoo SVD story and the Muon speedup claims. For me, this exercise shows both the strength and the risk of AI tools: they can quickly generate coherent, high-level practice that matches our lecture, but we still have to read critically, check details, and mark where the model is hand-waving or hallucinating.
