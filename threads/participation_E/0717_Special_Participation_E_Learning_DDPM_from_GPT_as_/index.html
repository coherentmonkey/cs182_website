Special Participation E: Learning DDPM from GPT as a tutor

By John Wang 2025-12-08 GPT
View on Ed Stem →
For this Special Participation E, I built an interactive DDPM (diffusion) tutor using a custom prompt in ChatGPT’s Study Mode. The tutor walks through the standard DDPM pipeline in four modules: (1) the forward noising process and the closed-form q(x_t | x_0), (2) the reverse process p_θ(x_{t-1} | x_t) and noise prediction ε_θ(x_t, t), (3) the simplified “MSE on noise” training objective that comes from the ELBO / Gaussian KLs, (4) the sampling loop (from x_T ~ N(0, I) back to x_0), plus simple variants like DDIM and classifier-free guidance. In each module, the tutor: explains the intuition and minimal formulas, asks me concept-check questions, and gives a slightly flawed statement for me to critique (e.g., “q(x_t | x_0) always has mean zero,” “perfect ε-prediction still produces blurry samples,” or “training only at t=1 is enough”). This makes the interaction feel more like a guided oral exam than just reading the DDPM paper again. It helped me straighten out the roles of q(x_t | x_0), p_θ, the noise schedule, and the training loss, and forced me to actively explain and defend my answers instead of passively skimming notes.
