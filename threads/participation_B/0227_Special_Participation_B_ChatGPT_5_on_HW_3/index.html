By Ben Yu 2025-11-04 HW3 GPT
View on Ed Stem →
What I Did: I tried to use GPT-5 to help me understand the muP coding lab (q_mup_coding.ipynb) -- especially parts (c)–(d), which involved per-layer learning-rate scaling and graph scaling factors. The conversation grew extremely long because I was confused about the correct scaling factor (√n_in, n_in, 1/√n_in, 1/n_in, √(n_out/n_in), etc.) and how these relate across RMS, spectral, and induced norms. Later the released solution notebook turned out to have an incorrect scaling line (x * p_shape[1] instead of x / p_shape[1]), which made it harder to verify correctness before the announcement came out. Workflow 1. Ask questions: I pasted the exact sub-questions: “What is part d’s scaling factor?”, “Should I divide by √ or n_in?”, “Where in code step() handles bias?”, etc. 2. Compare & Probe: Each time, I compared the AI’s answer to lecture notes. and when results disagreed, I asked for the missing algebra or an intuition (“Why does 1/fan-in appear?”). 3. Tighten understanding: I repeatedly asked it to rewrite arguments in plain words and to clarify the difference between the RMS→RMS induced norm and the spectral norm. Example of confusion: I kept alternating between x /= sqrt(layer.in_features) [fan-in normalization] and x *= sqrt(layer.out_features / layer.in_features) [μP graph scaling]. Both produced different plots, and ChatGPT changed answers multiple times and still did not converge to the correct forward scaling (divide by n_in). Observations Key insight: These factors solve different problems. For example, √(1/fan-in) keeps activations numerically stable; 1/fan-in (μP) keeps feature updates width-invariant under Adam. Positives (What Worked Well): Debugging guidance: Helped me trace the coding part, where step() applies to both weight and bias tensors and where the forward multiplier belongs. Negatives (Limitations / Effort Required): Concept collision: It mixed up “fan-in √ scaling” and “μP 1/n scaling” several times, making things more confused. Verification cost: Because the official solution had an error, I needed to cross-check the explanations to confirm the correct direction, which is hard since AI uses different wordings that I sometimes find hard to connect between two explanations Prompting Strategies That Helped “Elaborate / Why this?” → forced the model to expand the chain-rule or Jacobian steps. “Is my thought correct? If not, why?” → helped surface subtle contradictions. “Make a table.” → compressed multiple conflicting explanations into a clear comparative view.
