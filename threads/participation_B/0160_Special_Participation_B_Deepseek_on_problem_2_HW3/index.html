Deepseek conv: https://chat.deepseek.com/share/b9xx6hqmk42jzod0uu Transcript with comments: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1MWOQsiybYJ3Tgw99TATzzucOYQ1j57MV/view?usp=sharing For this special participation, I used DeepSeek to solve the Maximal Update Parameterization (μP) problem in Homework 3, evaluating its ability to reason about μP’s theory and implement its optimizer and network-scaling components correctly. DeepSeek performed strongly across Parts B–E, showing solid mathematical reasoning and clean, well-structured code, though occasionally verbose. In Part B, it identified the RMS→RMS induced norm as the key to explaining activation deltas and derived the correct relation ||W||_(RMS→RMS) = σ_max(W) * sqrt(n/m). Its explanation of why Frobenius and spectral norms fail to capture functional effects was clear and technically precise—one of its best moments. In Part C, DeepSeek implemented the SimpleAdamMuP optimizer with proper per-layer scaling (√(n_out / n_in)) and justified input/output layer “fudge” factors, accurately linking them to μP’s goal of uniform activation updates. Part D was the highlight, but also where its main weakness appeared. DeepSeek initially applied the wrong scaling (√(n_in / n_out)) and only realized the mistake after I asked why it had chosen that direction. It first tried to justify the incorrect reasoning before re-deriving and correcting itself to √(n_out / n_in). While it eventually reached the right conclusion and explained it well, this showed that it didn’t truly verify its own logic until prompted. Interestingly, I also noticed that it sometimes produced very smart intermediate reasoning during its “thinking” process but then left those insights out of its final answer. In Part E, it correctly demonstrated μP’s hyperparameter-transfer property—that a single global learning rate (≈1–3) generalizes across widths 4–256—capturing μP’s essence of “tune once, scale everywhere.” Overall, DeepSeek showed strong theoretical grasp, clean implementation, and genuine reflective reasoning. However, I found ChatGPT generally does a better job at maintaining coherence, verifying its logic, and integrating its best reasoning directly into the final answers. DeepSeek was technically solid but less self-consistent, making it feel more like an intelligent assistant that sometimes “knows” the right thing but doesn’t always say it.
