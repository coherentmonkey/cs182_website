Special Participation A: Deepseek on HW2

By Ken Zheng 2025-11-28 HW2 DeepSeek
View on Ed Stem →
I completed all non-coding parts of Homework 2 using Deepseek with DeepThink turned on. Motivation I’ve seen quite a few examples of classmates engaging in some light conversation with the model or using somewhat structured/designed prompts with the aim of improving the output quality and accuracy. I opted for a slightly different approach. I decided to use raw and "no-prompt" prompting, meant to mimic the setting where a student is treating the LLM less as an interactive tutor and more as a tool, a “calculator”, for problems. For every problem, my process consisted of highlighting the relevant subpart in its entirety on the homework PDF, then copy pasting into the text box. Nothing more. No context was given except for those present in the problem originally, and no prompt optimization was performed (i.e., no strategic "filler" words like "think carefully and answer accurately", "you are an expert in deep learning", "take a deep breath" [https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03409] were included). Summary Overall, Deepseek is a very capable model for reasoning tasks. It one-shots every non coding question in Homework 2. It arguably fails to notice one small detail in one of the subparts (see Q1 for more), but apart from that, all perfect. Analyzing the thinking trace reveals Deepseek's general chain of thought pattern, which is comprised of these steps: Restate the question Re-interpret the question with its own words List what is given/known Identify what is unknown/what the task is Formulate a strategy/plan of attack Carry out that strategy (i.e., start doing the work/calculations following the strategy) After finding answer, start doubting absolutely everything starting from step 1 to step 6. For every doubt, recheck work and see if other answers exist. If yes, explore those paths; else, move on to the next doubt. Return final answer after double-checking everything. People who took EECS 16A with Prof. Ranade (Fall 2023) might remember her teaching us a three step proof writing structure: What do we know? What are we trying to show? How can we get from 1 to 2? It is interesting to see Deepseek's thought habits closely follow this three step process (plus a plethora of double-checking), almost like Deepseek's frontier-level reasoning performance stems from the fact that it treats every technical question like completing a rigorous proof.
